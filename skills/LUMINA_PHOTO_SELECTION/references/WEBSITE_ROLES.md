# Website Roles

Assign selected images to practical website roles.

## Hero Selection Rule

Hero image must represent the emotional identity of the whole event, not only an individually strong image.

A valid hero should satisfy at least 3 of these:
- Shows the main subject, family, couple, monk, or event identity clearly
- Communicates the emotional tone of the event
- Works as a website opening image
- Has enough visual clarity for text overlay or layout placement
- Does not mislead the viewer about the type of event
- Is not merely a guest portrait, documentation shot, or technically clean filler

If an image is visually strong but does not represent the whole event, assign it as `featured`, `energy`, `detail`, or `gallery`, not `hero`.

## Hero Sequence

A short sequence of hero-capable images.

Avoid repeating the same distance, subject, or emotional note.

## Featured

Strong enough to represent the event, but not always the main hero.

## Emotional Anchor

The image that carries the emotional memory of the event.

It may be stronger than the most technically perfect image.

## Context

Shows place, situation, crowd, environment, or event scale.

## Detail

Adds texture and evidence. Useful for rhythm, not usually enough alone.

## Transition

Moves the story from one phase to another.

## Story Grid

Supports gallery rhythm. These images may be weaker alone but useful in sequence.

## Ceremony

Shows the formal or ritual center of the event.

## Closing

Ends the sequence with calm, confirmation, resolution, or emotional aftertaste.
